SOIL MOISTURE FORECASTING USING MULTI-SCALE TEMPORAL FEATURE FUSION AND ENSEMBLE MODELLING
AUTHORS: Rayyan Ahmed Minhas, Anas Pervaiz

ABSTRACT:
Soil moisture forecasting is important for irrigation planning, drought monitoring, and smart agriculture, yet accurate medium-horizon prediction remains difficult because soil conditions are influenced by both short-term weather variation and longer-term temporal persistence. This work presents a soil moisture forecasting framework based on multi-scale temporal feature fusion, where current observations are combined with short-, medium-, and long-range lagged signals to predict surface soil moisture 48 hours ahead. The dataset was constructed from ISMN sensor stations and enriched with environmental variables, temporal lags, rolling statistics, and calendar features. Multiple forecasting models were evaluated, including XGBoost, KNN, LSTM, Transformer, ARIMA, Prophet, and a stacking-based ensemble. The main novelty of the study is the use of a structured multi-scale lag design up to one week, together with ablation-oriented analysis to investigate which temporal horizons contribute most to forecasting performance. Experimental results show that the XGBoost-based temporal model achieved the strongest performance, with test RMSE of 0.0289, MAE of 0.0184, and R2 of 0.8566 across repeated runs. The study demonstrates that carefully engineered temporal context can provide a lightweight and effective alternative to heavier deep learning models for 48-hour soil moisture prediction.

PROBLEM STATEMENT:
Existing soil moisture prediction systems often struggle to deliver accurate medium-horizon forecasts while remaining lightweight, interpretable, and practical for real-world IoT-based agricultural settings. Many approaches either focus on short-term prediction, use limited temporal context, or rely on computationally expensive models without clearly analyzing which historical patterns are actually useful. This creates a need for an efficient forecasting framework that can exploit both short-term dynamics and longer soil moisture memory for reliable 48-hour prediction.

RESEARCH GAP:
